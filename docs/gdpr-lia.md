# Legitimate Interest Assessment (LIA) — Publishing coordinator submission data

**Status:** internal working document, for file only. Not published on the site.
**Scope:** the Art 6(1)(f) GDPR legitimate-interest basis relied on for
publishing approved BIP submissions (see `/privacy`, "Legal basis").
**Date:** 9th September 2026. **Review:** revisit if the published data set
changes (new personal fields) or if an objection under Art 21 is upheld.

## 1. Purpose — why we process

BipHub exists to maintain a free, public directory of Erasmus+ Blended
Intensive Programmes so students across Europe can discover them. That purpose
cannot be served without publishing the submission content coordinators provide
(programme title, description, dates, host institution, and the contact details
the coordinator chooses to publish). The interest pursued is BipHub's (via
Hexona Systems) in operating the directory, and — concretely — the interests
of students and participating institutions in finding and filling BIPs.

## 2. Necessity — is the processing actually needed for the purpose?

Yes. Publication is the product: an unpublished directory entry helps nobody.
We limit processing to what the purpose requires:

- Only **approved** submissions are published; drafts, pending, rejected, and
  changes-requested submissions are never public.
- Coordinators decide which contact details to publish at submission time.
- On account deletion, drafts and non-approved submissions are deleted and
  approved BIPs are **anonymized** (contact name and email removed), leaving
  only institutional information in the public directory.

There is no less intrusive way to run a public directory of who-to-contact
programme listings.

## 3. Balancing test — our interests vs. the coordinators' interests

**Nature of the data.** The published personal data is professional, not
private: a coordinator's name, work email, and institutional affiliation,
provided voluntarily in a work capacity for the express purpose of advertising
a programme. No special-category data (Art 9) and no children's data are
involved.

**Reasonable expectations.** A coordinator submitting a BIP for review knows
the outcome they are asking for is public listing — submission is an act
directed at publication, and approval emails confirm when the entry goes live.
Publication is therefore within their reasonable expectations.

**Relationship and power.** The coordinator is a professional acting for their
institution, not a vulnerable data subject; there is no imbalance of power
that publication could exploit.

**Safeguards.** Approval-gated publication; no publication of non-approved
content; anonymization of approved entries on account deletion; in-product
deletion path; and an explicit, separately stated right to object (Art 21) in
the privacy policy — an objection stops processing unless we demonstrate
compelling legitimate grounds or need the data for legal claims.

**Conclusion.** The legitimate interest in maintaining the public directory
outweighs the limited impact on coordinators, whose professional contact data
is published with their knowledge, for the purpose they submitted it, with
withdrawal and objection routes available. The Art 6(1)(f) basis is
appropriate. Record any Art 21 objections and their outcomes alongside this
document.
